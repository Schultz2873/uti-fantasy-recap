Union of Ten Idiots: Week 16 Recap
Fantasy Baseball Weekly Recap

Second Place, First-Class Beatdown

Me So Heorny humiliated the league’s No. 1 seed without taking the throne, three matchups became ended in blowouts, and BTA Boyz extended their historic march toward complete irrelevance.

By: Gunnarrhea Week: 16 Feature: Matchup-by-matchup recap

Week 16 was less of an extended matchup and more of an extended public execution. Me So Heorny remained in second place but completely embarrassed first-place BB’s Bold Team, Dixon Cider demolished BTA Boyz and extended their losing streak to 13 straight, and Goodyear Gila Monsters flattened the rapidly fading Mactown MacDaddies. You Don’t Know Bo continued climbing back into the playoff race by ending Gunnarrhea’s winning streak, while John’s Super Team and This Is Mizerable spent two weeks battling just to finish exactly where they started with an 8–8 tie. With the playoffs quickly approaching, the contenders are beginning to separate themselves from the frauds—and nobody’s seed feels completely safe anymore, except for whoever gets The BTA Boyz on their schedule.

Dixon Cider vs BTA Boyz

Result: Dixon Cider put BTA Boyz through the cider press.
Dixon Cider 14.5 – 1.5 BTA Boyz

Alright, let’s get this one out of the way because everyone knew how it was going to end. Dixon Cider demolished the dead weight known as BTA Boyz 14.5–1.5. Golly, was this a slaughter. Dixon could have set the lineup Monday morning, thrown the computer into Lake Michigan, and still won comfortably. Just look at the differences. Dixon finished with 60 more at-bats, crushed BTA 24–3 in home runs, nearly doubled them 68–29 in RBI, and buried them 211–121 in total bases. The pitching staff joined the domination with a 2.20 ERA, 1.016 WHIP, 125 strikeouts, nine wins, 11 quality starts, and a .203 BAA. This was never a matchup. It was Dixon Cider taking batting practice against an abandoned franchise.

BTA Boyz are officially donezo, and I should probably stop writing recaps for this team because it is the same shit every single week. I suppose the pitchers showing actually getting put in the active spot is one small positive, but the rest of the roster remains a complete disaster. The offense managed only three home runs, 29 RBI, 121 total bases, and a .232 average during an extended matchup. The losing streak has now reached 13 straight matchups, and there are absolutely no signs that anyone is attempting to stop it. This man is still holding two injured players who are not even employed by MLB organizations. What are we doing here, guy? Waiting to activate them for the 2030 celebrity softball game? Is the Fantrax app still downloaded? Does anyone remember the password? At this point, BTA is not rebuilding or tanking. The franchise has simply been abandoned with the lights left on. Figure it out, my guy.

League takeaway: Dixon Cider remains a championship threat. BTA Boyz remain proof that Fantrax will not automatically delete a team no matter how badly everyone wishes it would.

Gunnarrhea vs You Don't Know Bo

Result: You Don’t Know Bo avoided the infection and sent Gunnarrhea back to the clinic
You Don't Know Bo 11 – 5 Gunnarrhea

The winning streak continues for the 2025 GM of the Year as You Don’t Know Bo knocked off the red-hot Gunnarrhea 11–5 and avoided catching a nasty case. Surprisingly, Bo’s pitching staff didn’t completely shit itself for once. It actually dominated, posting a 2.80 ERA, 1.177 WHIP, and .241 BAA while winning five games. The usual power wasn’t there, as Bo hit only 11 home runs, but 120 hits, a .252 average, and eight stolen bases were enough to carry the offense. Bo is suddenly stacking wins and crawling back toward the playoffs like the greasy little cockroach nobody remembered to step on.

God, this is embarrassing for Gunnarrhea. Losing to the guy who looks like a sack of melted butter stings deep. The offense hit a revolting .205, struck out 143 times (YIKES), and produced only three stolen bases. Somehow, the pitching was even worse, vomiting up a 5.12 ERA, 1.428 WHIP, and .280 BAA while collecting eight losses. It’s fine, though. I had to donate Bo a victory before he lost interest and abandoned the league alongside BTA Boyz. Enjoy the charity win, butterball. Should we meet again in the playoffs, expect the good guys to administer a clean sweep.

League takeaway:

You Don’t Know Bo is officially back in the playoff hunt, while Gunnarrhea learned that even a sack of melted butter can be dangerous when your entire pitching staff decides to throw batting practice.

John's Super Team vs This is Mizerable

Result: John’s Super Team and This is Mizerable accomplished absolutely nothing together
John's Super Team 8 – 8 This is Mizerable

I’m sure this isn’t what John’s Super Team had in mind while trying to get the season back on track. An 8–8 tie isn’t a disaster, but it’s another week of standing completely still while the season runs out of runway. The frustrating part is that Johnboy’s offense had a legitimately strong week, finishing with 130 hits, 77 runs, 23 home runs, 70 RBI, a .277 average, and 218 total bases. He beat Mizerable in nearly every major offensive statistic and still couldn’t finish the job. This was one of the league’s best teams last season, and now John is sitting around .500 as the playoffs creep closer. The man simply does not possess the clutch gene. He can perform all the bicep curls he wants, but no amount of gym time will strengthen his ability to close out a fantasy matchup. The muscles may be growing, but the win total sure isn’t.

This is Mizerable will gladly accept any result that doesn’t add another loss to the record after spending most of the season renting out the basement. The offense wasn’t even bad, producing 120 hits, 20 home runs, a .274 average, and 204 total bases, but the pitching staff was the real reason Miz escaped with a tie. Over 95.2 innings, the staff delivered a ridiculous 1.88 ERA, 0.909 WHIP, 104 strikeouts, nine quality starts, and a .185 BAA. Even during an extended matchup, those numbers were absolutely disgusting. And to think, Miz nearly traded away the most dominant pitcher in the league at the beginning of the season. Imagine if BTA Boyz had actually landed him. The poor Miz would have spent half the year on the bench. That trade falling apart may have saved the pitcher’s season, Miz’s franchise, and the league’s remaining sanity.

League takeaway: John keeps lifting weights instead of victories, and Miz keeps being rescued by pitchers he was almost dumb enough to trade away.

Mactown MacDaddies vs Goodyear Gila Monsters

Result: Goodyear flattened Mactown and left the MacDaddies on the side of the road
Goodyear Gila Monsters 14 – 2 Mactown MacDaddies

It’s good to see Goodyear finally collect more than one quality start in a matchup. Apparently, last week’s loss irritated the Gila Monsters enough to make an example out of the rapidly deteriorating Mactown MacDaddies. Goodyear won 14–2, and I’m pretty sure the score was sitting at 16–0 at one point before Mactown salvaged two pity categories from the wreckage. The offense still wasn’t overwhelmingly powerful, finishing with only 16 home runs and a .240 average, but it produced 64 runs, 57 walks, seven steals, and 193 total bases. That was more than enough when the pitching staff delivered a 2.81 ERA, 0.958 WHIP, 108 strikeouts, seven wins, seven quality starts, 10 net saves, and a .198 BAA. Aaron Judge is still missing, yet Goodyear continues winning because the rest of the roster actually knows how to function. BTA Boyz you paying attention? The Gila Monsters remain one of the league’s most complete teams and a legitimate threat to crawl back into the finals.

Mactown MacDaddies, boy oh boy. The final score makes this look like the offense was completely useless, but the hitting was actually much closer than the 14–2 massacre suggests. Mactown hit .271, recorded 110 hits, drove in 66 runs, and finished only 12 total bases behind Goodyear. Unfortunately, the pitching staff once again arrived carrying gasoline and matches. Mactown stumbled through just 56.1 innings with a 4.95 ERA, only 54 strikeouts, one win, six losses, and three quality starts. That pitching staff has been the bane of this team’s existence all season, and every week it finds a new and exciting way to blow it. The good news is that eight teams make the playoffs, so Mactown still has time to assemble a few competent pitching matchups and crawl back into the picture. The even better news is that BTA Boyz have already padlocked themselves inside the 10th-place basement, so at least one spot is permanently unavailable.

League takeaway: Goodyear reminded everyone that last week’s loss was merely a temporary malfunction, while Mactown proved that a respectable offense means absolutely nothing when the pitching staff treats every opposing hitter like they purchased the premium batting-practice package.

Me So Heorny vs BB's Bold Team

Result: Me So Heorny exposed the league’s top seed and left BB completely limp.
Me So Heorny 14 – 2 BB's Bold Team

Ladies and gentlemen, drumroll please—it’s time for the supposed Matchup of the Week! The first seed against the second seed, two championship contenders battling over an extended matchup with league-wide bragging rights on the line. At least, that was how it was advertised. Instead, Me So Heorny beat the absolute brakes off BB’s Bold Team 14–2. Heorny dominated the offensive counting categories with 74 runs, 25 home runs, 71 RBI, 57 walks, seven net steals, and 211 total bases. The pitching wasn’t flawless with a 4.29 ERA, but it was still comfortably better than BB where it mattered, finishing with a 1.152 WHIP, 107 strikeouts, seven quality starts, five net saves, and a .242 BAA. Apparently, last week’s loss was taken personally because Heorny unloaded every ounce of frustration directly onto the league’s No. 1 seed. This wasn’t a statement victory—it was a UTI execution.

BB, are the rumors true? Are you already washed? Are you overrated? Have most of these victories been fraudulent little accidents held together by favorable scheduling and luck? Nobody can say for certain, but getting publicly dismantled 14–2 certainly didn’t beat the allegations. BB managed to win batting average at .263 and pitcher wins with eight, and then apparently decided that two categories were plenty. The offense was beaten 25–16 in home runs, 74–60 in runs, 71–57 in RBI, and 57–36 in walks. Meanwhile, the pitching staff coughed up a 4.76 ERA, 1.392 WHIP, and .260 BAA. This was BB’s chance to prove why they belong at the top of the standings, and instead they spent the extended week curled into the fetal position while Heorny harvested categories. The record may still say first place, but after this performance, the league is going to need to see some identification.

League takeaway: Me So Heorny proved it may be the team everyone should actually fear in the playoffs, while BB’s Bold Team turned the Matchup of the Week into a documentary about an overrated first seed getting exposed
